Odoo 19 Activities: manage next actions without losing ownership

An Odoo activity can look simple: call a customer back, approve an invoice, follow up with a supplier or prepare a decision. Yet in an Odoo business in Belgium or France, those actions quickly become invisible if their subject, due date and owner are not governed. Odoo 19 Enterprise improvements around the My Activities view, due-date filters and rescheduling make daily work easier; they do not replace an ownership rule.

Treat an activity as a work commitment

An activity should answer three questions: who acts, on what and by when? Avoid vague labels such as “check with customer”. State the expected outcome and attach the action to the right object when it exists: CRM opportunity, quotation, invoice, sales order, project or service intervention. An activity created without a document can be useful for capturing an emerging action, but it should be qualified or attached before it becomes a structured omission.

Use My Activities without creating a black box

Odoo 19 release notes state that a user can create activities from the My Activities view without initially requiring an object. That flexibility is valuable after a meeting or call. It also requires a routine: every team should decide when an unattached activity is completed, turned into a task or removed. Without that triage, the personal view collects intentions that give the business no usable visibility.

Make due dates a signal, not decoration

Odoo 19 adds due-date filters — tomorrow, this week and this month — and quick options for rescheduling an activity. For an SME using Odoo Belgium or Odoo France, these filters can support a daily and weekly routine. They do not determine whether a deadline is acceptable: define which activities are critical, when a delay must be visible to a manager and when an action should be reassigned.

Do not turn rescheduling into an automatic remedy. Moving a date is appropriate when the cause and new date are known. When a customer is waiting, an invoice is blocked or a production incident persists, the activity must trigger a decision, not merely change colour in a list.

Connect activities to real processes

Activities work across CRM, sales, accounting, purchasing and service. Map the few events that must always produce human follow-up: a quotation with no response, an exceptional approval, a missing document, a payment anomaly or a ticket closure. Then assign the business owner, due date and evidence of completion. An Odoo automation is useful only when the team knows what to do when it fires.

Acceptance-test rights, deadlines and exceptions

Underside fictional scenario: a services business operating in Brussels and Lille uses Odoo CRM and Invoicing. It creates an activity after a sales call and another linked to a disputed invoice, then checks each user’s view. The team tests due-date filters, rescheduling, assignment and recovery of an unattended activity. Above all, it confirms that the manager sees material delays without gaining unnecessary access to unrelated records.

  • Define a label, expected outcome, owner and date for every activity type.
  • Identify activities that must be linked to an Odoo document before they are closed.
  • Test personal views, due-date filters and rescheduling with real permissions.
  • Set a recovery rule for absences, overdue work and team changes.
  • Measure overdue activities by process without confusing action volume with service quality.
  • Document the activities that trigger management escalation or an accounting control.

Official source and scope

The official Odoo 19 release notes describe creation from My Activities, the new due-date filters and rescheduling options. The qualification, escalation and acceptance-testing rules are Underside analysis and must be adapted to your processes, permissions and customer commitments.
