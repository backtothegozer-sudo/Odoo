Odoo 19 Work Orders: reliable workshop execution

A routing only creates value when every operator knows what to do, where to do it and when to report an exception. For an Odoo business in Belgium or France, Odoo 19 Enterprise work orders connect a manufacturing order to operations, work centers and actual time spent.

Start with a routing people understand on the shop floor

Break production into operations with observable outcomes: cut, assemble, inspect, pack. Each operation should name a work center and include an instruction that a trained person can follow. Avoid generic labels that hide inspection steps, tooling changes or handovers between teams.

Make time data useful

Expected time should support decisions, not create a flattering metric. Separate preparation, execution and inspection time. Compare declared time with a sample of completed orders: an isolated average cannot explain a delay, breakdown or component shortage.

Sequence operations without losing exceptions

Work-order dependencies can block an operation until a previous step is completed. They are useful for curing, mandatory inspection or first-article approval. Define the normal case and who can authorise an exception.

Acceptance-test before rollout

Underside fictional scenario: a workshop in Namur and another in Lyon assemble the same product through preparation, assembly and inspection. The team launches a normal order, one with a missing component and one requiring rework. It checks instructions, sequence, declared time, permissions and how the variance is handled.

  • Test opening, starting and completing every work order.
  • Verify instructions, documents and units shown.
  • Simulate a blocked operation and validate the exception rule.
  • Compare expected time, declared time and variance reason.
  • Check partial production, scrap and rework.

Official source and scope

Odoo 19 documentation on work centers explains how to enable work orders. Acceptance-testing rules are Underside recommendations to adapt to your workshop.
