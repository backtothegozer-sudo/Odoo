Odoo 19 MTBF and MTTR: make decisions with maintenance metrics

Metrics do not repair a machine, but they make decisions less instinctive. For an Odoo business in Belgium or France, MTBF and MTTR are meaningful only when each request clearly distinguishes a failure, preventive work and return to service. In Odoo 19 Enterprise, this data is calculated on the equipment record: it can support a maintenance, replacement or capacity decision without replacing an on-site assessment.

Read metrics without over-interpreting them

Mean time between failures (MTBF) expresses an average interval, in days, between reported failures. Odoo calculates it from completed corrective maintenance and derives an estimated next-failure date from the latest failure. Mean time to repair (MTTR) is also calculated from the duration of prior requests. These values describe the history that was entered: incomplete data does not become reliable merely because it appears in a dashboard.

The expected mean time between failures can be configured on the equipment record. Treat it as an initial reference and retain its source, such as a manufacturer recommendation or earlier history. Do not change it to make a dashboard look more reassuring; compare the expected value, the observed value and the production context instead.

Make requests comparable

For useful calculations, name equipment unambiguously, connect it to the correct work center and record the same type of detail for every incident: symptom, date, duration, intervention, replaced part and outcome. A completed corrective request should not include a preventive inspection simply because it happened on the same day.

Assign both a team and an owner. A rising MTTR may point to an unavailable spare part, waiting time for access, difficult diagnosis or an uneven team workload. It does not prove any one of those causes by itself; the request note and related events provide the necessary explanation.

Move from a number to a workshop decision

Use the metrics by equipment family and criticality, rather than as an isolated machine ranking. An equipment record with a falling MTBF may justify a preventive review, a spare-parts decision or an analysis of operating conditions. A high MTTR for a constrained work center may call for a diagnostic procedure or planned downtime. In an Odoo Belgium or Odoo France project, the decision also needs to account for customer commitments, safety rules and production capacity.

Define the thresholds that trigger a review and who makes the call. Without this rule, the dashboard becomes a late observation. A short monthly review can be enough: equipment approaching estimated failure, repeated failures, lengthy repairs and requests without a documented cause.

Acceptance-test data quality before the dashboard

Fictional Underside scenario: a Belgian SME tracks a press assigned to a work center. The team creates two corrective requests with realistic durations, then a separate preventive request. It checks the metrics shown on the equipment, the latest-failure date and the consistency between request information and the scheduling decision. It does not approve a replacement threshold until the history is representative.

  • Create equipment with an effective date, team and responsible person.
  • Set and document the expected mean time between failures.
  • Record completed corrective requests with comparable durations and causes.
  • Create a planned preventive intervention separately.
  • Check MTBF, MTTR, latest failure and estimated next failure.
  • Have the workshop review a sample of requests before using the metrics in a management meeting.

Official source and scope

Official Odoo 19 documentation on maintenance setup describes the equipment metrics and their calculation. The proposed thresholds, review rules and acceptance scenarios are Underside’s analysis: adapt them to your equipment, safety constraints and processes.
